Coward's Arsenal

Original Concept & Narrative Design · Concept

The Premise

A coward raised by assassins escapes the organization that shaped his life. Haunted by fragmented memories of a future ending in destruction, he must grow strong enough to gather powerful allies—and survive what is coming.

Worldbuilding

The protagonist begins inside an assassin organization whose training has taught him exactly how outmatched he is. Beyond it wait a mysterious master, powerful individuals connected to the coming catastrophe, and outside entities whose influence is still being defined.

This is a concept in development. The catastrophe, the organization, and the wider quest structure are deliberately presented as working material rather than finished canon.

The Protagonist — A Coward Who Refuses to Die

He is motivated first by survival and comfort, not glory. Training begins as a practical attempt to earn the attention of people who might help him live through the future he remembers. His memories return gradually because his mind cannot handle the full burden of his previous life at once.

The planned arc asks whether survival instincts can grow into a willingness to protect and lead others. That arc remains a design intention, not a completed character journey.

Design Process

01 — Character Design

Decision: The protagonist is genuinely cowardly and begins by wanting survival and a comfortable life rather than heroic recognition.

Reason: He understands the gap between his own strength and that of the world's most powerful people. That self-awareness makes his fear practical rather than performative.

Player impact: The player should read his choices as attempts to manage risk. Gathering allies becomes a believable extension of his personality, not an arbitrary quest objective.

Trade-off: A survival-first protagonist can seem passive if the story does not give him active decisions. The planned arc must therefore show him choosing when to trust, train, and eventually protect others.

02 — Narrative Structure

Decision: Fragmented memories unlock gradually instead of revealing the whole future at once.

Reason: His mind cannot handle the full burden of his previous life at once, and gradual recovery lets the mystery progress alongside his ability to interpret it.

Player impact: The player discovers the future as a changing, incomplete problem. Each recovered fragment can revise their understanding of the catastrophe without requiring the complete reveal sequence to be finalized now.

Trade-off: Delayed information can feel vague or repetitive if every memory only withholds answers. Each planned reveal needs to change the protagonist's available choices or relationships.

03 — Player Experience

Decision: Use observation, hiding, environmental interactions, and avoidance of stronger enemies to express his survival-first mindset.

Reason: The player's actions should communicate his character more directly than exposition alone. A stealth or evasion choice makes his fear part of play.

Player impact: The player should feel tension without assuming that every encounter is a combat test. Success comes from reading danger and choosing a survivable route.

Trade-off: Avoidance-focused play can become frustrating if it removes agency. Encounters need readable patrols, clear feedback, and more than one viable way to create distance.

04 — Open Questions and Next Steps

Decision: Keep unresolved story elements visible instead of presenting them as finished canon.

Reason: The Seven Sins, the cause of the catastrophe, and the protagonist's eventual relationships with the people he recruits still need development.

Player impact: The finished structure should make those uncertainties feel like mysteries the player can investigate, not gaps hidden behind confident exposition.

Trade-off: Leaving the questions open preserves flexibility but delays commitments that will shape the quest campaign. The next step is to define the first polished quest and use it to test the protagonist's voice, stakes, and relationship-building.

Quest Structure

  1. Escape the assassin organization while avoiding pursuers.
  2. Train under a master and learn what strength might make possible.
  3. Recover fragments of a dangerous future and identify the people connected to it.
  4. Build relationships and alliances before the catastrophe arrives.

Quest Sample: The Price of Running

Objective: Escape a dangerous area while avoiding pursuers. The player observes patrols, uses environmental cover, and chooses when to move.

Character expression: The protagonist avoids direct confrontation because he knows he is outmatched. A failed attempt alerts the patrol and returns the player to the last safe position.

Narrative payoff: The escape reinforces his survival-first mindset and establishes the threat posed by his former organization.

Design Rationale

Gradual memory recovery keeps the mystery playable and lets the protagonist's understanding change with the player's. The regression claim remains unreliable, creating room to question what the memories mean.
