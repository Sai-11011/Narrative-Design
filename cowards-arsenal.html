Original Concept / Narrative Design

Coward's Arsenal

A survival-first protagonist, fragmented memories of a ruined future, and the difficult work of turning fear into a reason to build alliances.

Dark Fantasy·Narrative RPG·Concept in Development
CASE STUDY 01

01 / The Core Idea

The Premise

A coward raised by assassins escapes the organization that shaped his life. Haunted by fragmented memories of a future ending in destruction, he must grow strong enough to gather powerful allies—and survive what is coming.

His first goal is not to become a hero. He wants to stay alive, gain enough strength to be taken seriously, and eventually live comfortably. Building a team begins as a survival strategy: the coming threat is too large for him to face alone.

!

Development status
This is an original concept in development. The catastrophe, the wider quest structure, and several character relationships are still being worked out; this page distinguishes current ideas from proposed design directions.

02 / World & Stakes

A Future He Cannot Fully Explain

The protagonist has regressed and remembers fragments of a future that ends in destruction. He tells people about his regression openly, but most do not believe him. His memories return gradually because, after his first regression, his mind cannot handle the entire influx at once.

The threat is connected to powerful outside entities and seven individuals who may become suitable vessels. Their personal stories and circumstances are not yet fully defined. The protagonist's current direction is to understand them and seek a way to save them rather than simply treating them as enemies.

Known direction

Fragmented future memories drive the protagonist to prepare for a catastrophe.

Open question

The exact cause of the catastrophe and the Seven Sins' individual stories remain in development.

03 / Character Design

A Coward Who Refuses to Die

The protagonist is genuinely cowardly, not secretly fearless. He understands how far below the world's strongest people he is, and that awareness shapes the risks he is willing to take. His instinct is to avoid a fight he cannot win.

Training under a powerful master and gathering allies are practical decisions before they are heroic ones. He needs enough strength and influence that the people he hopes to recruit will take him seriously. Whether this survival instinct can grow into a willingness to protect others is an intended character arc, not a completed journey.

“

His fear is not a flaw the story needs to erase. It is the starting point that gives his choices meaning.

04 / Design Process

Turning Story Intent into Play

These are design intentions and trade-offs for the concept, not claims about completed or tested gameplay.

01

Character Through Decisions

Decision
Make survival and comfort the protagonist's initial priorities rather than heroic recognition.
Reason
He knows how outmatched he is. His fear should follow from his understanding of the world's power gap.
Intended player effect
Risk management and alliance-building feel like extensions of his personality, not arbitrary objectives.
Trade-off
A survival-first lead can feel passive unless the player is given meaningful decisions about when to hide, train, trust, or act.
02

Fragmented Memory as Structure

Decision
Let memories return gradually instead of giving the protagonist a complete account of the future at once.
Reason
His mind cannot handle the full influx of memories after his first regression.
Intended player effect
The player and protagonist can piece together the coming threat over time.
Trade-off
Partial revelations may feel repetitive if they only delay answers. Each reveal should ideally change what the protagonist understands or chooses.
03

Survival Expressed Through Play

Decision
Explore observation, hiding, environmental interaction, and avoiding stronger enemies as ways to express his mindset.
Reason
Player actions can communicate fear and caution more directly than exposition alone.
Intended player effect
Encounters become problems to read and navigate, rather than assuming every threat must be defeated in combat.
Trade-off
Avoidance can become frustrating without readable threats, clear feedback, and meaningful choices. These would need to be validated through prototyping and playtesting.
04

Keep Unresolved Elements Visible

Decision
Present the Seven Sins, the catastrophe, and future relationships as open development questions rather than settled canon.
Reason
Their individual stories need further development before the larger quest structure can be finalized.
Intended player effect
In the eventual game, uncertainty should become something the player investigates—not a gap hidden by confident exposition.
Next step
Develop one focused quest to test the protagonist's voice, stakes, and survival-first choices before expanding the campaign.

06 / Proposed Quest Sample

The Price of Running

A proposed scene concept for demonstrating how character intent could inform an encounter. This is not implemented gameplay or finalized story canon.

Objective

Escape a dangerous area while avoiding pursuers.

Player approach

Observe patrols, use environmental cover, and choose when to move instead of assuming direct combat is the best answer.

Failure & recovery

If spotted, the pursuers close in and the attempt returns to the last safe position. This is a proposed recovery rule, not a tested implementation.

Narrative purpose

Reinforce the protagonist's survival-first mindset and establish the threat posed by the organization he left.

✦

Design question to test

Can the encounter make caution feel like an active, satisfying choice rather than a punishment for not fighting? A prototype would need to test threat readability, route choice, and how failure is communicated.
