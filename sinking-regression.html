Original Concept / Narrative & Game Design

Sinking Regression

A botanist abandoned beneath the sea must turn unfamiliar plant life into a way out—while every death returns them to the beginning with their memories intact.

Underwater Puzzle·Science-Fiction Mystery·Draft / Work in Progress
CASE STUDY 02

01 / The Core Idea

Stranded Below the Surface

A botanist is betrayed by jealous colleagues and cut loose underwater. With escape seemingly impossible, they search the surrounding environment for plants and biological materials that could help them construct a bio-submarine.

Death does not simply end the attempt. The protagonist regresses to the beginning, retaining memories of previous runs. Each return gives them another chance to understand the environment, test a new approach, and uncover what happened in this place.

!

Development status
The premise and regression hook are established; the full puzzle sequence, exact rules of regression, and ending remain open design work. This page describes intended ideas, not a completed or playtested game.

02 / Intended Player Loop

Learn, Experiment, Return

The proposed loop connects environmental discovery with the mystery of regression.

  1. 01

    Explore

    Investigate underwater spaces and identify plants, resources, and signs of earlier activity.

    Concept
  2. 02

    Experiment

    Discover which biological materials could help create or improve a means of escape.

    Concept
  3. 03

    Fail and Regress

    When a run ends, return to the beginning while retaining knowledge from the previous attempt.

    Core Hook
  4. 04

    Reinterpret

    Use accumulated knowledge to try a different route, solve a puzzle, or investigate a clue that previously made little sense.

    Concept
✦

Design intention

Regression should make failure informative. The player's knowledge—not just a larger inventory or stronger character—should enable progress on a later attempt.

03 / Narrative Mystery

What Was Left Behind?

The protagonist's immediate goal is survival, but repeated attempts reveal a larger mystery. Skeletons and discarded experiments suggest that others may have been trapped here before. Their presence raises questions about the facility, the research, and the true nature of regression.

Established premise

The protagonist is betrayed, stranded underwater, and searching for a biological route to escape.

Mystery to develop

Why regression occurs, who came before, and what the abandoned experiments reveal.

The mystery is intended to unfold through investigation rather than a single exposition dump. The exact clue sequence still needs to be designed.

04 / Design Questions

Turning the Hook into Play

01

Make Knowledge Matter

Goal
Let discoveries from previous attempts change what the player can understand or accomplish in the next run.
Risk
If every attempt repeats the same tasks without new information, regression may feel like busywork.
Next step
Prototype one short puzzle where a failed attempt reveals a clue that enables a different solution.
02

Connect Plants to Puzzles

Goal
Make botanical knowledge useful to exploration and construction, not merely a collectible checklist.
Risk
Players may not understand a plant's use if its properties are communicated too vaguely.
Next step
Design a small set of readable plant properties and test whether players can infer a useful combination.
03

Tell the Story Through the Environment

Goal
Use abandoned equipment, skeletons, and experimental remains to create questions the player wants to answer.
Risk
Environmental details become decoration if they do not affect the player's interpretation or decisions.
Next step
Build one compact investigation with a clue and a meaningful discovery.

05 / Proposed First Prototype

One Room, One Regression

A small prototype could test the central idea without requiring the entire underwater world.

Space

One contained underwater area with a clear exit objective.

Interaction

One discoverable plant or biological resource with a useful property.

Puzzle

A simple obstacle that requires learning from an earlier attempt.

Regression

Restart the attempt while preserving knowledge, then let the player use it to progress.

Validation

Observe whether players understand what they learned and why the next attempt is different.

Next step
Define the exact regression rules and build one repeatable puzzle before expanding the story, map, or crafting system.
